# Content, voice, and proof

Use this reference for sitemap planning, draft copy, message hierarchy, calls to action, and case studies.

## Build a message spine

Before writing sections, define:

- one plain-language promise;
- the audience and situation;
- the strongest supported differentiator;
- the proof needed to believe it;
- the primary action and what happens after it.

Every section should answer a new buyer question. Remove sections that exist only to lengthen the page.

## Truth states

Mark draft content in working notes as `VERIFIED`, `PROVISIONAL`, `INFERRED`, or `BLOCKED`. Plausible copy is still provisional. Never turn an inferred benefit into a quantified result.

When facts are missing, prefer neutral, useful placeholders such as “Add approved service area” or a clearly labelled sample project. Do not fabricate believable names, quotes, dates, logos, or numbers.

## Voice

Extract vocabulary from actual client material and customer language. Define a few traits and their limits: direct not blunt, warm not sugary, technical not opaque. Use concrete nouns and verbs. Avoid generic phrases such as “elevate,” “unlock,” “seamless,” “bespoke solutions,” and “where quality meets innovation” unless quoting approved brand language.

## Headings and CTA labels

Headings should carry information, not merely announce categories. CTA labels should name the action: “Request a survey,” “Check availability,” “View the menu,” or “Book a consultation.” Keep one label per intent across navigation, hero, and footer.

## Proof

Prioritise observable evidence: real work, process detail, named people, materials, policies, accredited status, sourced reviews, and outcomes. Decorative metrics are not proof. Client logos require permission and a real relationship.

## Case studies

Record context, problem, constraints, role, decisions, work, and supported outcome. If outcomes are not available, describe the delivered change without inventing impact. Use images with captions that explain what the visitor is seeing.

## Final copy audit

Read every visible string, including metadata, alt text, form errors, buttons, captions, nav, and footer. Check grammar, referents, consistency, unsupported claims, accidental placeholder text, fake precision, and tone drift. Plain accurate copy is better than clever nonsense.
