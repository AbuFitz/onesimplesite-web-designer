# Client website strategy

Use this reference for marketing websites whose job is to create enquiries, bookings, calls, visits, or qualified trust.

## Model the conversion

State the conversion in concrete terms: “request a survey,” “book an initial call,” “check availability,” “view recent kitchens,” or “get directions.” “Learn more” is usually navigation, not the business outcome.

Match CTA commitment to visitor readiness. A high-trust or high-cost service often needs evidence, process, and a lower-friction first step before “buy now.” A time-sensitive local service may need a visible telephone route immediately.

## Homepage content pool

Select and order only what helps this buyer decide:

- clear offer and service area;
- fast route to the primary action;
- visual or factual proof near the promise;
- outcomes and differentiators expressed concretely;
- services organized in the customer’s language;
- selected work, case studies, menu, rooms, treatments, or other category evidence;
- process that reduces uncertainty;
- named people and credentials where personal trust matters;
- reviews with genuine attribution and permission;
- practical objections: timing, coverage, access, pricing approach, guarantees;
- location, hours, contact options, and final next step.

Avoid empty sections added only to make the page longer. Repetition should deepen confidence, not restate the same claim.

## Copy standard

- Lead with the customer’s concrete need and the business’s specific answer.
- Prefer evidence to superlatives: materials, method, years, response model, named expertise, before/after detail.
- Use the words customers use when searching, without writing for a keyword robot.
- Vary sentence length and write headings that convey information.
- Keep the company’s actual tone; do not make every client sound like a venture-backed software brand.
- Mark provisional content clearly in the handoff. Never let believable draft copy masquerade as a verified fact.

## Trust by business type

Adjust the evidence mix:

- **Trades/home services:** service area, response expectations, accreditations, insurance, workmanship, project photos, review source, direct call route.
- **Professional practice:** named expertise, regulated status, confidentiality, scope boundaries, process, plain-language insight, appropriate disclaimer.
- **Hospitality:** atmosphere, location, menu/rooms, availability route, accessibility, policies, current imagery, maps and opening times.
- **Creative/portfolio:** selected work, role and contribution, outcomes, process, fit, restrained contact path.
- **Health/wellbeing:** practitioner credentials, informed expectations, contraindications or boundaries, privacy, accessibility, careful claims.

Only include items supported by the client.

## Local and technical findability

When relevant, provide unique page title and meta description, one clear page topic, logical headings, crawlable business details, consistent name/address/phone, service-area wording, descriptive internal links, canonical and social metadata, favicon, robots/sitemap support, and appropriate structured data based on verified facts. Do not add fake review ratings or unsupported schema.

## Forms and contact paths

- Ask only for information the business will use.
- Use persistent labels, suitable input types, autocomplete, inline validation, clear errors, keyboard focus, and a meaningful success state.
- Explain what happens after submission and any response expectation only if known.
- Do not claim a form works until its endpoint and delivery have been tested.
- Provide a non-form alternative when the business and user context call for it.

Depending on scope, consider service detail pages, work/case studies, about, contact, privacy, terms, accessibility information, and a useful 404. Do not manufacture legal text as professional advice; use client-approved or jurisdiction-appropriate content.
